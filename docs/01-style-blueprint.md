# 01. Style Blueprint

Honest note: this blueprint comes from the channel's stated goal plus common conventions of this genre. It is NOT a frame-by-frame analysis of his videos (I cannot watch video). Use the worksheet in section 6 to confirm each point.

---

## 1. The format in one line

One idea per video, built from first principles, shown as simple moving diagrams, narrated calmly by someone who actually does the work.

His stated goal (paraphrased from his About page): walk viewers through how he thinks about core computer-systems ideas, using visualization to make them clear.

---

## 2. Video structure (target 8 to 12 min)

| Block | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00 to 0:30 | A real failure or a sharp question. No intro, no logo, no "hey guys" |
| Problem | 0:30 to 2:00 | Show the simplest version of the problem on screen |
| Naive solution | 2:00 to 4:00 | Build the obvious fix, then break it with an animation |
| Better solution(s) | 4:00 to 9:00 | Each fix answers the failure from the previous step |
| Recap | 9:00 to 10:00 | One diagram that shows the full idea |
| Close | last 15 sec | One question or the next video. No long outro |

Rule: every section must answer the question raised by the section before it. This is what keeps people watching.

---

## 3. Visual rules

- Flat dark background, 3 to 4 colors only
- One color = one meaning (example: blue = healthy node, red = failure, yellow = message)
- Basic objects only: boxes, circles, arrows, small packets, timelines, tables
- Max 5 to 7 objects on screen at once
- Animate only what the narration is saying right now
- Something changes on screen every 20 to 40 seconds
- Text on screen: labels only, no paragraphs
- Same easing and same speed for the same kind of motion
- Zoom or highlight to direct the eye. Dim everything else
- Resolution 1920x1080, 30 fps (60 fps only if motion looks choppy)

---

## 4. Audio rules

- Calm, clear, conversational. No hype, no shouting
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence
- Natural pauses before and after key moments. Pauses give the eye time to follow
- Start at 140 to 160 words per minute, adjust to your natural pace
- Little or no music. If used, very low
- Details in file 05

---

## 5. Titles and thumbnails

- Title patterns: "How X really works", "Why X breaks", "What happens when X"
- Thumbnail: one clean diagram + 3 to 5 words. No faces needed
- Promise one clear outcome. Deliver it in the first minute

## 7. How you stay original (not a copy)

- Different topics (your .NET, Azure, SQL Server experience)
- Your own palette and your own icon set
- Your own voice and your own examples from real work
- Same teaching style, different content

---

## 8. Quality bar before publishing

- [ ] Can a viewer explain the idea back in one sentence?
- [ ] Is there a visual change at least every 40 seconds?
- [ ] Any sentence that needs a rewrite after reading aloud?
- [ ] Any on-screen text that can be removed?
- [ ] Audio clean, no clicks, no loud breaths?
