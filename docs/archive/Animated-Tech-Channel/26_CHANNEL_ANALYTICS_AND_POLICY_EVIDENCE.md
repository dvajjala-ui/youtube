# 26 — Post-Publish Learning, AI Disclosures and Originality

## Measure the actual audience response
| Metric | What it answers | First action if poor |
|---|---|---|
| First 30-second retention | Did the promise/hook work? | Fix opening, clarify stakes, remove preamble |
| Retention dips and spikes | Where did clarity or interest change? | Rewatch the marked segment; don't assume every spike is positive |
| Average view duration / % viewed | Did pacing support a full explanation? | Shorten redundant setup, move payoff earlier without sacrificing accuracy |
| CTR by traffic source | Does packaging attract the right people? | Test more truthful title/thumbnail alternatives |
| Returning viewers | Do viewers come back for the style/topic? | Build a coherent repeatable series |
| Comments that ask “why?” | Where did the explanation fail? | Add diagram, context or corrected follow-up |

**Don't promise fixed CTR/retention targets.** Compare videos of similar length, topic and distribution source before making decisions. YouTube's audience retention interface identifies intros, spikes and dips (where enough data is available).

## Platform testing
- YouTube officially supports desktop A/B testing of up to 3 titles/thumbnails for eligible long-form videos and chooses by **watch-time share**, not simply CTR. Availability and video eligibility vary.
- Match the title/thumbnail claim to the actual content; curiosity must not become deception.
- Export Shorts separately from original masters with clear 9:16 compositions and legible labels.

## Monetization and reuse
- Don't mass-produce interchangeable videos using minor text/scene changes.
- The channel must deliver original educational value; internally generated script/animation/code alone does not guarantee YPP approval.
- Keep descriptions with a factual source list, original-story framing, and asset credits when required.
- Never present a fictional customer outage as a verified real company incident.

## AI disclosure (as of Nov 2026)
- YouTube requires disclosure for certain realistically generated or materially altered AI media, such as footage of a real scene/event or a real person doing something they did not do.
- Stylized, clearly non-realistic animation and AI assistance with outlines/scripts generally do **not** require the same disclosure solely for being AI-assisted.
- Assess **each episode and its voice/footage** against current Studio requirements before upload. For ambiguous cases, be transparent and check official docs.
