# 11 — Channel Launch, Monetization and Metrics
## Account setup
- Create an original, searchable channel name and logo. Check YouTube handle/trademark availability before choosing; no one has reserved a name through this kit.
- About page: original animated stories about backend, cloud, security and technical incidents; disclose reenactments clearly.
- Enable 2-step verification; configure YouTube Studio channel basics, language, upload defaults and thumbnail styling.
- Make a dedicated folder for licenses, original assets and source logs.

## Publishing package (per episode)
- One conflict-led title; thumbnail with one dominant object or failure and 2–4 words maximum (guideline).
- Description: 2-sentence premise, clear note when dramatized, chapter timestamps, primary educational sources, correction contact.
- English subtitles, original high-resolution thumbnail, chapters, end screen link to next related video.
- Shorts: one educational insight, not an uncredited excerpt of somebody else's video.
- Publish **manually** after rights, factual and disclosure checks.

## YouTube Partner Program (general current public requirements)
- Ads/YouTube Premium: 1,000 subscribers **and** either 4,000 qualified long-form public watch hours in past 12 months **or** 10 million qualified Shorts views in past 90 days, plus eligibility and review.
- An earlier expanded tier can permit selected fan-funding features at 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads and required hours/Shorts views; it **does not** mean full ad revenue is active.
- Platform reviews channel authenticity. Repetitive/mass-produced generic AI content, copied or minimally transformed videos are a monetization risk.
- YouTube may require labels for realistically AI-altered people/events/places; ordinary non-realistic animated educational diagrams generally differ. Read current settings and policy for every upload.
- Revenue is **not guaranteed**; geography, RPM, watch time, advertiser suitability and viewer mix change outcome. Never treat one channel's public view counts as evidence of earnings.

## Test and improve — first four videos
Track impressions; click-through rate (CTR); average view duration; retention at 0:30, 1:00, first technical explanation and root-cause reveal; returning viewers; subs gained; traffic sources. Avoid treating one small sample as statistically conclusive.

### Initial hypotheses, not benchmarks
- If early retention drops sharply: cold open may be confusing or slow.
- If viewers stop at the diagrams: too much jargon or visuals too dense.
- If CTR weak but watch retention strong: title/thumbnail likely a better test target.
- If watch retention weak and CTR high: expectation mismatch or script problem.

## 30-day experimental target
- Publish **one polished pilot**, then begin second script, rather than promise a viral cadence.
- Run 2–3 title/thumbnail concepts offline. After publication, record data at day 1, 7 and 28; change one production variable for episode 2.

## Earnings model examples (pure scenarios)
`Revenue ≈ monetized video views ÷ 1,000 × actual RPM`.
- 100k views × ₹50 RPM → ₹5,000
- 100k views × ₹150 RPM → ₹15,000
- 1M views × ₹150 RPM → ₹1,50,000
**These are arithmetic examples, not RPM predictions or claims about Pouria.** Before YPP and eligible ad views, ad revenue to creator may be zero. Factor in taxes, subscriptions, time cost and sponsorship terms.
